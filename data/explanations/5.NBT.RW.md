# Read, write, compare and order whole numbers to billions; expanded form (5.NBT.RW, 5.NBT.A.1 ext.)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Big numbers are read in groups of three digits called periods: billions, millions, thousands, ones. Commas separate the periods.
2. Read each period like a three-digit number, then say the period's name: 2,046,300,015 = "two billion, forty-six million, three hundred thousand, fifteen".
3. Zeros are place holders: they keep the other digits in the right place even though you do not say them.
4. Expanded form writes the value of each non-zero digit and adds them: 830,207,000 = 800,000,000 + 30,000,000 + 200,000 + 7,000.
5. To compare numbers with the same number of digits, compare from the left, one place at a time; the first difference decides.

## Worked example
Order 4,090,000; 4,900,000; 4,009,000; 4,099,000. All start with 4 million. Look at the hundred-thousands: 0, 9, 0, 0 → 4,900,000 is largest. Among the rest, ten-thousands: 9, 0, 9 → 4,009,000 is smallest. Then thousands: 4,090,000 has 0, 4,099,000 has 9. Order: 4,009,000 < 4,090,000 < 4,099,000 < 4,900,000.

## Faded example (he completes)
Write in digits: "four hundred six thousand, twenty-nine". Thousands period: 406. Ones period: 029 (three digits!). Number: ___ . (Answer: 406,029.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Dropping a placeholder zero: writing 406,29 or 46,029. Scaffold: "Each period has exactly three digits — pad with zeros."
- Comparing by "which has more nines". Scaffold: "Start from the left. Same? Move one place right."
- Expanded form with the wrong place value (30,000,000 written as 3,000,000). Scaffold: count the places after the digit.
- Saying "and" between periods. Scaffold: in English number names, "and" is only for decimals.

## Representation sequence
1. Words: period names as a chant.
2. Picture: a place-value chart with period columns coloured.
3. Table: digit | place | value, then add for expanded form.
4. Story: population of countries / distance to the Moon in metres.
5. Teach it: he dictates a number in words and checks the coach's digits.

## Check by explaining back
"How do you decide which of two 7-digit numbers is bigger?"

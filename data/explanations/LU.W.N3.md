# Transitional words for sequence; concrete words and sensory details (LU.W.N3, W.5.3c-d)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Transitions are signposts that show order and time: first, then, next, after that, meanwhile, as soon as, finally, the next morning.
2. Use them to move between events and between scenes; without them the reader gets lost.
3. Concrete words are exact: "fox darted" instead of "animal ran"; "cinnamon" instead of "nice smell".
4. Sensory details use the five senses; two or three well-chosen ones beat a list of ten.
5. Check a paragraph: circle the transitions (are there any?), underline vague words (replace them), tick the senses used.

## Worked example
"We packed the car. We drove to Gabala. We set up the tent. It started to rain." → "First we packed the car, and then we drove north to Gabala. As soon as we had set up the tent, fat drops of rain began to drum on the canvas." Transitions: first, then, as soon as. Concrete: north, fat drops, drum, canvas. Senses: sound and touch.

## Faded example (he completes)
Add a transition and a sensory detail: "The party ended. The classroom was quiet." → "The party ended late, and ___ morning the classroom ___ (sound or sight detail)." (Example: the next; smelled of chalk and felt very quiet.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- "Then… then… then…". Scaffold: "Vary the signposts — after that, meanwhile, finally."
- Vague nouns and verbs (thing, went, nice). Scaffold: "Which exact thing? How exactly did it move?"
- Overloading with adjectives ("the big red shiny fast new car"). Scaffold: "Two precise details."
- Senses as a list. Scaffold: "Put the detail into a sentence with an action."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the signpost list; the five senses.
2. Picture: a road with signposts between scenes.
3. Table: vague word | concrete replacement.
4. Story: he describes the school canteen with three senses and two transitions.
5. Teach it: he shows a younger child how "animal" becomes "fox" and why it is better.

## Check by explaining back
"What do transition words do for the reader, and what makes a detail 'concrete'?"

# Expand, combine and reduce sentences for meaning, interest and style (LU.S.1, L.5.3a)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Expand: add detail that answers when, where, how, why: "The bird sang" → "At dawn, the small bird sang sweetly from the fig tree."
2. Combine: join two short sentences with a comma + and/but/so, or with a joining word that shows the relationship: because (reason), when/after (time), although (contrast).
3. Reduce: cut words that add nothing: "at this point in time" → "now"; "are of the opinion that" → "think".
4. Style: vary sentence length — a long sentence followed by a short one makes the short one hit harder.
5. Read it aloud: if you run out of breath or stumble, revise.

## Worked example
"Sabina opened the box. It was empty. She was surprised. She had expected a gift." Combine and reduce into two sentences: "When Sabina opened the box, she was surprised to find it empty. She had expected a gift." The time word "when" links the first two ideas; the short second sentence explains the surprise.

## Faded example (he completes)
Combine to show the reason: "Kamran was late. His bus broke down." → "Kamran was late ___ his bus broke down." Now expand "The children played" with a when and a where: "___, the children played ___." (Answers: because; After school / in the courtyard — any sensible choice.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Combining with "and… and… and". Scaffold: "What is the relationship — reason, time, contrast? Use that word."
- Comma splice when combining. Scaffold: "Comma needs a partner: and, but, so."
- Expanding with empty words ("very very big"). Scaffold: "Add a detail, not a volume knob."
- Reducing until the meaning is lost. Scaffold: "Say the sentence back — is anything important missing?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: three verbs — expand, combine, reduce — and what each does.
2. Picture: a sentence "skeleton" with slots for when/where/how/why.
3. Table: short sentences | joining word | combined sentence.
4. Story: he takes three lines from his own writing and improves each with one move.
5. Teach it: he shows a younger child how two toy-block sentences click together with a joining word.

## Check by explaining back
"When you join two sentences, how do you choose the joining word?"

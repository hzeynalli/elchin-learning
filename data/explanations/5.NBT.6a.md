# Relate multiplication and division; use the inverse to check (5.NBT.6a, 5.NBT.B.6 intro)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Multiplication and division undo each other. If 8 × 651 = 5,208, then 5,208 ÷ 8 = 651 and 5,208 ÷ 651 = 8.
2. A division has three parts: dividend ÷ divisor = quotient. Divisor × quotient = dividend.
3. To check a division, multiply the answer by the divisor. If you get the number you started with, the division is right.
4. If there is a remainder: divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend.
5. To find a missing dividend, work backwards: multiply, then add the remainder.

## Worked example
Kim says 5,208 ÷ 8 = 651. Check: 651 × 8. 600 × 8 = 4,800; 50 × 8 = 400; 1 × 8 = 8. Total 5,208. That equals the dividend, so Kim is right. If she had said 641, the check gives 5,128 ≠ 5,208 — wrong.

## Faded example (he completes)
A number divided by 7 gives 43 remainder 2. Number = 7 × ___ + ___ = ___ + 2 = ___ . (Answers: 43; 2; 301; 303.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Checking a division with another division. Scaffold: "Undo it — what is the opposite of dividing?"
- Forgetting to add the remainder back. Scaffold: "The remainder was left over — put it back."
- Multiplying the dividend instead of the quotient (5,208 × 8). Scaffold: "Multiply the ANSWER by the divisor."
- Trusting the calculation without checking. Scaffold: "You are not done until the check matches."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: "divide, then multiply back".
2. Picture: fact triangle — 5,208 on top, 8 and 651 at the bottom corners.
3. Table: dividend | divisor | quotient | remainder | check.
4. Story: 5,208 sweets shared into 8 bags; multiply back to count them.
5. Teach it: he makes up a division, gets the coach to "answer" it wrongly, and catches the mistake with a check.

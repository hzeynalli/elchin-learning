# Verb tense to convey time, sequence, state and condition (LU.G.3, L.5.1c)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. The verb tense is the time stamp of a sentence: past (walked), present (walk / walks), future (will walk).
2. Time words are clues: yesterday, last week → past; every day, usually → present habit; tomorrow, next year → future.
3. Sequence: when two past actions happen one before the other, the earlier one uses had + past participle (past perfect): "By the time the bell rang, Leyla had finished."
4. State: the present perfect (has/have + participle) shows something that started in the past and is still true: "I have lived in Baku for ten years" (still here). Simple past means it is finished.
5. Condition: "if … will …" says what would happen only if something else happens: "If it rains, we will stay inside."

## Worked example
"Every summer my cousins ___ to Sheki, and they ___ there again next year." First blank: a habit → simple present: go. Second: "next year" → future: will go. Full sentence: "Every summer my cousins go to Sheki, and they will go there again next year."

## Faded example (he completes)
"Kamran ate breakfast and left for school." Show that eating happened first: "Kamran ___ ___ breakfast before he left for school." (Answer: had eaten.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Using the present for a past story ("Yesterday he goes"). Scaffold: "Find the time word. When did it happen?"
- Irregular past forms (eated, goed). Scaffold: keep a list — eat/ate, go/went, run/ran.
- Using simple past for a sequence where past perfect is needed. Scaffold: "Which happened first? That one gets 'had'."
- Present perfect vs simple past for state. Scaffold: "Is it still true now?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: three time stamps, then the two special forms (had…, has/have…).
2. Picture: a timeline with now in the middle; place each sentence on it.
3. Table: time word | tense | example.
4. Story: he tells what he did yesterday, does every day, and will do tomorrow — one sentence each.
5. Teach it: he explains to a younger child why "I have lived" and "I lived" are different.

## Check by explaining back
"How does the verb tell the reader when something happened?"

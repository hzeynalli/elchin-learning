# Dialogue, description and pacing to develop experiences and characters (LU.W.N2, W.5.3b)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Dialogue shows who a character is by what they say and how: "Um… do we have to go in?" shows nerves without the word "nervous".
2. Description uses the senses to put the reader in the place: smells, sounds, light, texture.
3. Pacing is speed: slow down the important moment with small details and thoughts; speed up the boring parts by skipping them.
4. Show, don't tell: replace "he was angry" with an action — "He slammed the book shut."
5. Every line of dialogue needs a reason: it reveals character, moves the story, or raises tension.

## Worked example
Telling: "Kamran was angry." Showing: "Kamran slammed his book shut and stared at the wall. 'Fine,' he said. 'Do it yourself.'" The slam and the short cold sentence show the anger. Pacing a tense moment: "He reached for the handle. It was cold. Slowly, slowly, he turned it. Something moved on the other side."

## Faded example (he completes)
Show that Leyla is nervous before a test, without the word nervous: action ___ ; one line she says ___ ; one sensory detail of the room ___ .

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Naming the feeling. Scaffold: "What would I SEE her do?"
- Dialogue that only says hello. Scaffold: "What does this line tell me about the character?"
- Everything at the same speed. Scaffold: "Where is the most important moment? Slow it down there."
- Description lists without sentences ("cold, dark, quiet"). Scaffold: "Put the details into the action."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: show, don't tell; slow the big moment.
2. Picture: a speed line under the story — where does it slow, where does it race?
3. Table: feeling | action that shows it | line of dialogue.
4. Story: he rewrites one "told" sentence from his own writing as a shown moment.
5. Teach it: he acts out a feeling for the coach to guess, then writes the actions he used.

## Check by explaining back
"How can you show a character is worried without using the word?"

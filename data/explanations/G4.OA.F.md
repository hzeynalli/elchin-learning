# Multiplication and division facts to 12 × 12, fluent (G4.OA.F, 3.OA.C.7)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Fluent means fast and correct without counting: under 3 seconds a fact. That comes from strategy first, then practice.
2. Anchor facts: ×2 (double), ×5 (half of ×10), ×10, ×11 (repeat the digit), and the squares (6×6, 7×7, 8×8, 9×9).
3. Derive the rest from anchors: 7 × 8 = 7 × 4 doubled = 28 × 2 = 56; 6 × 9 = 6 × 10 − 6 = 54; 12 × 7 = 10 × 7 + 2 × 7 = 84.
4. Every multiplication fact gives two division facts: 7 × 8 = 56 → 56 ÷ 7 = 8 and 56 ÷ 8 = 7.
5. Practise the hard six daily: 6×7, 6×8, 7×8, 7×9, 8×9, 6×9. Say them, don't count.

## Worked example
9 × 9: an anchor square, 81. 11 × 12: 11 × 12 = 10 × 12 + 12 = 120 + 12 = 132. 8 × 4: double 8 twice: 16, 32. 56 ÷ 7: "7 times what is 56?" 7 × 8 — so 8.

## Faded example (he completes)
7 × 9 = 7 × 10 − ___ = 70 − ___ = ___ . So 63 ÷ 7 = ___ . (Answers: 7; 7; 63; 9.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Counting on fingers in threes/sevens — correct but slow. Scaffold: "Which anchor is closest? Derive from it."
- Mixing 7 × 8 = 54 / 6 × 9 = 56 (swapped). Scaffold: "56 = 7 × 8: five, six, seven, eight."
- Freezing when timed. Scaffold: practise untimed until correct, then add the timer gradually.
- Treating division facts as separate. Scaffold: "Turn it into a times question."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: fact families said aloud; the anchor list.
2. Picture: an array (7 rows of 8 dots) split into 7 × 4 and 7 × 4.
3. Table: the 12 × 12 grid with the anchors coloured; he fills the rest from them.
4. Story: 8 teams of 7 players; 56 players into teams of 8.
5. Teach it: he teaches the coach a trick for one hard fact.

## Check by explaining back
"How do you get 7 × 8 fast if you forget it?"

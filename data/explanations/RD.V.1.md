# Context clues to find word meaning (RD.V.1, L.5.4a)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Do not stop at a hard word: read the whole sentence and the one before and after. The clues are around it.
2. Clue types: a definition or restatement ("evaporation, which means…"), an example, a contrast ("but", "unlike"), a cause and effect ("because she…, she felt…"), and the general feeling of the sentence.
3. Replace the word with your guess and reread. Does the sentence still make sense?
4. Check with the part of speech: is it a thing, an action, or a describing word?
5. Only after that, use a dictionary — and choose the meaning that fits the sentence.

## Worked example
"They scrambled to the bank, dripping and ashamed, while the birds laughed from the trees." Clues: they just fell in after a silly fight; birds are laughing at them; "dripping" is a feeling-of-embarrassment situation. Guess: ashamed = embarrassed about what they did. Replace and reread: "dripping and embarrassed" — makes sense. Part of speech: describing word. Confirmed.

## Faded example (he completes)
"Her candle flickered twice and went out." Clue: it "went out" straight after. So "flickered" probably means the flame ___ . Replace: "the candle ___ twice and went out." (Answers: wobbled / shook unsteadily; wobbled.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Guessing from the look of the word (ashamed → "a shame" → "a pity"). Scaffold: "Use the sentence, not the letters."
- Skipping the word and never coming back. Scaffold: "Circle it, finish the sentence, then go back."
- Picking a dictionary meaning that does not fit ("bank" = money place). Scaffold: "Reread with that meaning — does it work?"
- A guess with the wrong part of speech. Scaffold: "Is it an action here, or a thing?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the five clue types as a checklist.
2. Picture: the hard word in a box with arrows from the clue words around it.
3. Table: word | clues | guess | reread check.
4. Story: he invents a nonsense word in a sentence and the coach works out what it means from clues.
5. Teach it: he shows a younger child how to "read around" a word.

## Check by explaining back
"What do you do when you meet a word you don't know?"

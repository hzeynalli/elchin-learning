# Compare and contrast characters, settings, events using details (RD.RL.3, RL.5.3)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Compare = how they are alike. Contrast = how they are different. Most questions want both.
2. Pick a feature to compare (what they want, how they act, where they are, what they say) and find a detail for each side.
3. Use the text, not your imagination: "Sabina trained every evening" vs "Kamran built a windproof lantern".
4. Sentence frames: "Both … but …", "While A …, B …", "Unlike A, B …".
5. When a character changes, compare the same character at the beginning and at the end.

## Worked example
Sabina and Kamran. Feature: strengths. Sabina: "She could run the entire length of the beach without pausing." Kamran: "clever with his hands and patient with details." Alike: both want to reach the tower with a lit lantern. Different: her strength is speed; his is craft. Sentence: "While Sabina is the faster runner, Kamran is the better builder, and in the end they need both."

## Faded example (he completes)
Tofiq at the beginning: he ___ in the shade and ___ at Nara. Tofiq at the end: he is the ___ ant through the door and carries ___ seeds. Sentence: "At first Tofiq …, but by spring he …". (Answers: lies / rests; laughs; first; two.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Listing facts about each without linking them. Scaffold: "Use 'but' or 'while' to join the two."
- Comparing different features (her speed vs his kindness). Scaffold: "Same feature on both sides."
- No evidence. Scaffold: "Which sentence shows that?"
- Only differences. Scaffold: "Name one thing they share first."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the sentence frames.
2. Picture: a Venn diagram — shared in the middle.
3. Table: feature | character A (quote) | character B (quote).
4. Story: he compares two people he knows using the same table.
5. Teach it: he explains a Venn diagram to a younger child with two animals.

## Check by explaining back
"Give me one way the two characters are alike and one way they are different, with a detail for each."

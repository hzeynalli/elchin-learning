# Reading habit: chooses books, sets goals, reads 20+ minutes daily, keeps a log (RD.HAB, QSI Reading E01)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Readers get better by reading — 20 minutes every day is the single strongest habit for reading scores. The app tracks passages; books count too.
2. Choosing a book: use the five-finger rule — read one page; if five or more words are unknown, it is too hard for now; if none, it may be too easy for growth.
3. Set a goal you can measure: "20 minutes a day, 5 days a week" or "finish this book by Friday".
4. Keep a log: date, title, pages or minutes, one sentence about what happened or what you thought. The log is for you, not for marks.
5. Talk about what you read: tell someone one thing you liked and one question you have.

## Worked example
Elchin picks a book about the sea. Page one has two unknown words — a good fit. Goal: 20 minutes after homework, Monday to Friday. Log entry: "9 Sep — The Sea Book, 14 pages. Whales sing to each other across the ocean. Question: how far can the sound travel?" Weekly review with a parent: streak of days, one favourite moment.

## Faded example (he completes)
Write today's log line: date ___, title ___, minutes ___, one sentence ___, one question ___ .

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Choosing books that are far too hard (or only comics). Scaffold: five-finger rule; mix easy and stretch.
- Goals like "read more". Scaffold: "How many minutes, how many days?"
- Log entries that are just titles. Scaffold: "One sentence about the story, one question."
- Reading 60 minutes once a week instead of 20 daily. Scaffold: "Daily is the magic; a little every day."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the habit rule — 20 minutes, every day, a book you chose.
2. Picture: a calendar chain — do not break the chain.
3. Table: the reading log itself.
4. Story: he recommends a book to the coach in three sentences.
5. Teach it: he explains the five-finger rule to a younger child.

## Check by explaining back
"How do you choose a book that is right for you, and how do you know you are keeping your goal?"

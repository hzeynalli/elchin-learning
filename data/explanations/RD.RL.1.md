# Quote accurately from a text; draw inferences with evidence (RD.RL.1, RL.5.1)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Two kinds of questions: "right there" (the answer is written in the text) and "think and search" (you combine clues to infer).
2. Routine for every question: read the question, find the evidence, quote it, then answer. Evidence first, answer second.
3. Quoting means copying the exact words inside quotation marks. Paraphrasing is saying it in your own words — different job.
4. An inference is a sensible conclusion from clues plus what you already know. It must be supported by something in the text.
5. Say the sentence that connects them: "The text says '…', so I can tell that …".

## Worked example
Question: How do we know the goats regretted their fight? Find: "'We could easily have done that,' said the white goat in a quiet voice." Quote it. Infer: a quiet voice and "could have" show he wishes they had acted differently. Answer: They regretted it; the quote shows it.

## Faded example (he completes)
Question: Was Tofiq sorry? Evidence sentence: "'You were right,' he admitted quietly." Quote: ___ . Inference: the word ___ and the word ___ show he feels ___ . (Answers: the sentence; admitted; quietly; sorry / ashamed.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Answering from memory of the story instead of the text. Scaffold: "Put your finger on the sentence."
- Quoting too much (a whole paragraph) or too little (two words). Scaffold: "One sentence that proves it."
- Changing words inside the quotation marks. Scaffold: "Copy exactly, comma for comma."
- Inference with no clue: "I think he was sad because I would be." Scaffold: "Which words in the text made you think that?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the four-step routine as a chant.
2. Picture: highlight the evidence in one colour, the answer in another.
3. Table: question | evidence quote | my inference.
4. Story: a detective — clues (quotes) lead to a conclusion (inference).
5. Teach it: he writes a "think and search" question about a passage for the coach to answer with a quote.

## Check by explaining back
"What is the difference between quoting and answering in your own words? When do you need each?"

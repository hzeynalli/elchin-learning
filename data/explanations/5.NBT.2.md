# Powers of 10 with exponents; multiply and divide by powers of 10 (5.NBT.2, 5.NBT.A.2)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. 10 × 10 × 10 is written 10³. The small number (the exponent) counts how many tens are multiplied.
2. 10³ = 1,000: a 1 followed by 3 zeros. The exponent tells you the number of zeros.
3. Multiplying by 10ⁿ moves every digit n places to the left (the number gets bigger); the empty places fill with zeros.
4. Dividing by 10ⁿ moves every digit n places to the right (smaller).
5. For 30 × 400: multiply the front digits (3 × 4 = 12), then attach the zeros from both numbers (1 + 2 = 3 zeros) → 12,000.

## Worked example
37 × 100: 100 = 10², so every digit moves two places left: 37 → 3,700. Check: 37 hundreds is 3,700. And 4,500 ÷ 10²: move two places right: 4,500 → 45.

## Faded example (he completes)
6 × 10³: 10³ = ___, so 6 × ___ = ___. Then 30 × 400: 3 × 4 = ___, zeros to attach: ___, answer ___. (Answers: 1,000; 6,000; 12; 3; 12,000.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Reading 10³ as 10 × 3 = 30. Scaffold: "The exponent counts tens multiplied, not added."
- "Add a zero" for ×10 works for whole numbers but fails later with decimals. Scaffold: "digits move places; say it that way from now on."
- Losing a zero in 30 × 400 (getting 1,200). Scaffold: "Count the zeros in both numbers before you start."
- Confusing which way to move for ÷. Scaffold: "Dividing makes it smaller, so the digits move right."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: "the exponent counts the zeros".
2. Picture: place-value chart with digits sliding left or right by n boxes.
3. Table: 10¹, 10², 10³, 10⁴ with their values and zero counts.
4. Story: 3 boxes of 400 grams; 30 boxes of 400 grams.
5. Teach it: he explains why 6 × 10³ has more zeros than 6 × 10².

## Check by explaining back
"What does the 4 in 10⁴ tell you? What happens to 37 when you multiply it by 10⁴?"

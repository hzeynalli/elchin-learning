# Plan, draft, revise, edit with a checklist; task, purpose, audience (LU.W.P1, W.5.4–5)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Before writing, decide the task (what kind of text), the purpose (to entertain, explain, persuade) and the audience (who will read it). These shape length, words and tone.
2. Plan: a short list — characters, setting, problem, three events, ending — or for non-fiction, the main points in order.
3. Draft: write it through without stopping to fix spelling. Get the ideas down.
4. Revise: change ideas and organisation — add detail, cut what does not help, reorder, improve the opening and ending. Use a checklist.
5. Edit: fix spelling, punctuation, capitals, tense. Read aloud to catch mistakes.
6. Revising and editing are different jobs done at different times.

## Worked example
Draft: "We went to the zoo. It was fun. We saw animals. Then we went home." Revision note: add specifics and feelings — which animals, what happened, why it was fun. Revised: "At the zoo, a baby elephant sprayed water over the fence and soaked my sister's shoes. We laughed until the keeper asked us to move on." Edit: check capitals and full stops. Audience change: for six-year-olds, shorter sentences and a happy ending.

## Faded example (he completes)
Plan a story using the checklist: narrator ___; setting ___; problem ___; events 1 ___ 2 ___ 3 ___; ending ___ .

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Editing spelling while drafting, losing the idea. Scaffold: "Ideas first, spelling later."
- "Revising" that only fixes commas. Scaffold: "Did any IDEA change?"
- No plan, so the story wanders. Scaffold: the five-line plan.
- Same style for every audience. Scaffold: "Who is reading this? What do they need?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the five steps as a chant — plan, draft, revise, edit, share.
2. Picture: a staircase with the five steps; a checklist on the revise step.
3. Table: draft sentence | revision question | improved sentence.
4. Story: he revises one paragraph of his own school writing with two revision questions.
5. Teach it: he explains to a younger child the difference between revising and editing using a drawing (change the picture vs clean the smudges).

## Check by explaining back
"What is the difference between revising and editing, and which comes first?"

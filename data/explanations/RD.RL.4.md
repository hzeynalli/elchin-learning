# Meaning of words and phrases, including figurative language — metaphor and simile (RD.RL.4, RL.5.4)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Literal language means exactly what it says. Figurative language paints a picture to make you feel or see something.
2. A simile compares using "like" or "as": "as flat and heavy as a brick."
3. A metaphor says one thing IS another: "the lanterns were small moons" — they are not really moons.
4. To explain a figurative phrase, ask: what two things are compared, and what do they share? Then say the plain meaning.
5. Writers use them to show how bad, how big, how quiet — with feeling.

## Worked example
"A loaf as flat and heavy as a brick." Two things: the loaf and a brick. Shared: flat, heavy, hard. Plain meaning: the bread failed completely. It is a simile because of "as … as". Why the author chose it: a brick is the opposite of what bread should be, so we feel Rasim's disappointment.

## Faded example (he completes)
"Their lanterns glowing like miniature moons." Two things compared: ___ and ___ . Shared: ___ . Simile or metaphor? ___ because of the word ___ . (Answers: lanterns; moons; round, glowing, small in the dark; simile; like.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Taking it literally ("the bread was made of clay"). Scaffold: "Is the writer telling a fact, or making a picture?"
- Mixing simile and metaphor. Scaffold: "Is there a 'like' or 'as'?"
- Explaining the comparison but not the meaning. Scaffold: "So what is the writer really saying about the bread?"
- Idioms ("busy being right") read word by word. Scaffold: "What does the whole phrase mean together?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the two definitions with the test words like/as.
2. Picture: draw the literal picture (a brick loaf) next to the real meaning.
3. Table: phrase | two things | shared | plain meaning | simile or metaphor.
4. Story: he describes his school day with one simile and one metaphor.
5. Teach it: he explains to a younger child why "the classroom was a zoo" is not about animals.

## Check by explaining back
"What is the difference between a simile and a metaphor? Give me one of each."

# Place-value relationships: 10× and 1/10 of adjacent places (5.NBT.1, 5.NBT.A.1)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Every place in a number is worth ten times the place to its right. Hundreds are ten times tens; thousands are ten times hundreds.
2. Going the other way, every place is one tenth of the place to its left.
3. The same digit can be worth very different amounts: in 5,550 the left 5 is 5,000, the middle 5 is 500, the right 5 is 50.
4. To compare two digits, write their values and ask "how many times bigger?" 5,000 ÷ 500 = 10.
5. This is why moving a digit one place left multiplies its value by 10, and one place right divides by 10.

## Worked example
In 5,550: the 5 in the hundreds place is worth 500; the 5 in the thousands place is worth 5,000. 5,000 is 10 times 500, so the thousands 5 is 10 times the hundreds 5. The hundreds 5 is one tenth of the thousands 5.

## Faded example (he completes)
In 7,070: the 7 in the tens place is worth ___; the 7 in the thousands place is worth ___. ___ ÷ ___ = ___, so the thousands 7 is ___ times the tens 7. (Answers: 70; 7,000; 7,000 ÷ 70 = 100; 100 times — two places apart.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Saying "the 5s are the same". Scaffold: "Same digit — is it in the same place? What is each one worth?"
- Answering 2 or "two places" instead of 100 for two places apart. Scaffold: "10 for one step, 10 × 10 for two steps."
- Mixing up ×10 and ÷10 direction. Scaffold: "Left is bigger. Which way did you move?"
- Reading 1/10 as 10. Scaffold: write the fraction and the value side by side.

## Representation sequence
1. Words: "each place is ten of the one to its right".
2. Picture: a place-value chart with the digit written in two columns; arrows labelled ×10 / ÷10.
3. Table: place | value of the digit | ratio to the next place.
4. Story: money — a 10-manat note is ten 1-manat coins; a 100-manat note is ten 10s.
5. Teach it: he explains to a younger child why 5,000 is "ten of 500".

## Check by explaining back
"What happens to a digit's value when it moves one place to the left? And to the right?"

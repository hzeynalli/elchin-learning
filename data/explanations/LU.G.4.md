# Recognise and correct inappropriate shifts in verb tense (LU.G.4, L.5.1d)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. A piece of writing usually stays in one tense. If a story starts in the past, it stays in the past unless the time really changes.
2. An inappropriate shift is when the tense changes for no reason: "She opened the door and walks in."
3. To check: underline every verb, write P (past), Pr (present) or F (future) above it, and look for odd ones out.
4. Fix the odd one by matching it to the others — usually the first verb sets the tense.
5. A shift is correct when the time really changes: "Yesterday I lost my key, but I will look for it tomorrow."

## Worked example
"Last Saturday we visit the museum. The guide showed us a golden helmet, and everyone gasps. Then we ate lunch in the garden." Verbs: visit (Pr), showed (P), gasps (Pr), ate (P). "Last Saturday" sets the past. Fix: visit → visited; gasps → gasped.

## Faded example (he completes)
"Murad picks up the ball, ran to the goal and scores." Tenses: picks (___), ran (___), scores (___). Choose one tense for all three: ___ . Rewrite: ___ . (Answers: Pr, P, Pr; present or past; "Murad picks up the ball, runs to the goal and scores" / "picked, ran, scored".)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Fixing only one verb and leaving another. Scaffold: "Mark every verb first, then fix."
- Changing the wrong one (making everything present in a "last Saturday" story). Scaffold: "Which time word is in the sentence?"
- Thinking every change of tense is a mistake. Scaffold: "Did the time actually change? Then it is allowed."
- Reported speech: "She said she will come." Scaffold: "After 'said', will becomes would."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: mark P / Pr / F above the verbs.
2. Picture: a timeline; verbs that jump off the line are suspects.
3. Table: verb | tense | matches the others?
4. Story: he writes four sentences about a match in the past, then checks them with the marks.
5. Teach it: he finds the shift in the coach's deliberately wrong sentence and explains it.

## Check by explaining back
"How do you find a verb that has changed tense by mistake?"

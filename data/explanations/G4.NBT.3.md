# Multiply up to 4-digit by 1-digit and 2-digit by 2-digit (G4.NBT.3, 4.NBT.B.5)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Break the big number by place value and multiply each part: 3,246 × 7 = 3,000×7 + 200×7 + 40×7 + 6×7.
2. The column method does the same thing right to left with carrying: 6 × 7 = 42, write 2 carry 4; 4 × 7 = 28 + 4 = 32, write 2 carry 3; and so on.
3. Two-digit × two-digit: split the second number into tens and ones, get two rows, add them. 46 × 23 = 46 × 20 + 46 × 3.
4. Estimate first: 3,246 × 7 ≈ 3,000 × 7 = 21,000; 46 × 23 ≈ 50 × 20 = 1,000.
5. In word problems, find the two numbers that multiply and ignore numbers that are not needed.

## Worked example
46 × 23. Row 1 (ones): 46 × 3 = 138. Row 2 (tens): 46 × 20 = 920. Add: 138 + 920 = 1,058. Estimate check: about 1,000. Reasonable.

## Faded example (he completes)
3,246 × 7 by columns: 6 × 7 = 42 → write 2, carry ___. 4 × 7 = 28 + ___ = ___ → write 2, carry 3. 2 × 7 = 14 + 3 = ___ → write 7, carry 1. 3 × 7 = 21 + 1 = ___ . Answer: ___ . (Answers: 4; 4; 32; 17; 22; 22,722.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Adding the carry before multiplying. Scaffold: "Multiply first, THEN add the carry."
- In 2×2, multiplying only the ones (46 × 3) and stopping. Scaffold: "23 is 20 and 3 — where is the 20 row?"
- Forgetting the 0 in the tens row. Scaffold: "You are multiplying by 20, so the row ends in 0."
- Using a distractor number from the story. Scaffold: "Which numbers answer the question? Cross out the rest."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: "split by place, multiply each, add".
2. Picture: area model — a 46 × 23 rectangle cut into 40×20, 40×3, 6×20, 6×3.
3. Table: partial products listed and summed.
4. Story: 23 boxes of 46 pencils.
5. Teach it: he draws the area model for a younger child.

## Check by explaining back
"Why are there two rows when you multiply by 23?"

# Writer's notebook habit; generates ideas; sets writing goals (LU.W.ID, QSI Writing E01)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Writers collect before they write. A notebook catches ideas, overheard lines, odd facts and small observations before they disappear.
2. Ideas come from noticing: a man feeding pigeons with one hand while reading a letter; a shop with a blue door. Ask "what if?" and "why?" to turn an observation into a story.
3. Keep entries short — a line or two is enough. Date them.
4. Set one writing goal at a time and make it checkable: "use dialogue in every story this month", "write ten minutes every day", "stop using the word very".
5. Look back every week: which idea could become a story? Is the goal being met? What is the next goal?

## Worked example
Entry: "Saw an old man feeding pigeons with one hand while reading a letter with the other. He looked worried." What if the letter is from someone he has not seen in fifty years? Why is he worried? Story seed: an old man, a letter, a decision to make by sunset. Goal for the month: "Every story I write will have a real ending that shows a change." Check: reread each story's last paragraph on Friday.

## Faded example (he completes)
Write three story ideas in one line each, each with a character and a problem: 1 ___ 2 ___ 3 ___. Choose one and write a goal for the week: ___ ; how you will check it: ___ .

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Waiting for the perfect idea. Scaffold: "Write the small thing you noticed today. That is enough."
- Goals like "write better". Scaffold: "How many? How often? How will you know?"
- Notebook entries that are just titles. Scaffold: "One line about what happened, one question."
- Never rereading the notebook. Scaffold: Friday five minutes — circle one idea.

## Representation sequence
1. Words: notice → note → ask what if → goal.
2. Picture: the notebook page with date, observation, question.
3. Table: date | observation | what-if | possible story.
4. Story: he turns one of his own entries into a three-sentence story seed.
5. Teach it: he shows a younger child how to keep a "noticing" list.

## Check by explaining back
"How do you turn something you noticed into a story idea, and what makes a writing goal a good one?"

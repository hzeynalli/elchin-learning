# Divide up to 4-digit by 1-digit with remainders (G4.NBT.4, 4.NBT.B.6)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Division asks "how many groups of this size?" 2,957 ÷ 6: how many sixes in 2,957, and what is left over?
2. Long division works place by place from the left: thousands, hundreds, tens, ones. At each step: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down.
3. If a digit is too small to divide, write 0 in the answer and bring down the next digit.
4. Whatever is left at the end and is smaller than the divisor is the remainder. Write it as "492 r 5".
5. Check: divisor × quotient + remainder = the number you started with.

## Worked example
2,957 ÷ 6. 2 thousands: too small for 6 → look at 29 hundreds. 6 × 4 = 24, write 4, 29 − 24 = 5, bring down 5 → 55. 6 × 9 = 54, write 9, 55 − 54 = 1, bring down 7 → 17. 6 × 2 = 12, write 2, 17 − 12 = 5. No more digits: remainder 5. Answer 492 r 5. Check: 6 × 492 = 2,952, + 5 = 2,957.

## Faded example (he completes)
1,384 ÷ 4. 1 is too small; 13 ÷ 4 = ___ r 1; bring down 8 → 18 ÷ 4 = ___ r 2; bring down 4 → 24 ÷ 4 = ___ . Answer ___ . (Answers: 3; 4; 6; 346.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Forgetting the 0 in the quotient when a digit is too small (writing 46 instead of 406). Scaffold: "Every digit you bring down needs a digit in the answer."
- Remainder bigger than the divisor. Scaffold: "If the leftover is 6 or more, one more group fits."
- Not checking. Scaffold: multiply back before saying you are done.
- In word problems: rounding the wrong way (buses: round up; full boxes: round down). Scaffold: "What does the remainder mean here?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — "Does McDonald's Sell Burgers?"
2. Picture: sharing base-ten blocks into 6 groups, trading when needed.
3. Table: the long-division layout step by step.
4. Story: 2,957 sweets into 6 bags; how many each, how many left?
5. Teach it: he narrates each step aloud for a younger child.

## Check by explaining back
"What do you do when the digit you bring down is smaller than the divisor?"

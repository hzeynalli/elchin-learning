# Capitalisation; punctuating dialogue with quotation marks and commas (LU.M.6, L.4.2 review)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Capital letters: the first word of a sentence; names of people and places; days, months, holidays; titles like Mr; the word I. Not ordinary nouns (aunt, museum, book) and not seasons.
2. Dialogue: the words a person says go inside quotation marks: "Let's go," said Murad.
3. A comma separates the speech from the speaker tag, and it sits inside the closing quotation mark: "Let's go," — not "Let's go",
4. Question and exclamation marks replace the comma and also stay inside: "Can we go?" asked Aysel.
5. The speech starts with a capital letter. When the tag comes first, put the comma after it: Aysel asked, "Can we go?"
6. A new speaker starts a new line.

## Worked example
"the coach shouted keep running elchin" → The coach shouted, "Keep running, Elchin!" Capital T (first word), comma after shouted, opening marks, capital K, a comma before the name (direct address), exclamation mark inside the closing marks.

## Faded example (he completes)
aysel asked can we go to the park → ___ asked, "___ we go to the park___" (Answers: Aysel; Can; ?" — question mark inside the marks.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Capitalising every important-sounding noun ("my Aunt", "the Museum"). Scaffold: "Is it the actual name of one particular person or place?"
- Comma outside the quotation marks. Scaffold: "The comma lives inside the house."
- No comma between speech and tag. Scaffold: "Speech, comma, tag — or tag, comma, speech."
- Lower-case start inside the quotation. Scaffold: "Speech starts like a sentence."
- Interrupted speech: "If we leave now," said Nigar, "we will catch the bus." — the second part is lower case because it continues the sentence. Scaffold: "Is it one sentence split in two?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the capital-letter list; the "comma inside" rule.
2. Picture: draw the quotation marks as a house; the punctuation is furniture inside.
3. Table: sentence | what needs a capital | what needs marks/commas.
4. Story: he writes a four-line conversation between two characters from a passage.
5. Teach it: he corrects the coach's deliberately unpunctuated dialogue.

## Check by explaining back
"Where does the comma go when someone speaks, and why does the speech start with a capital?"

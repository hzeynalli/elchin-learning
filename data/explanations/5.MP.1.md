# Choose a strategy, show working, estimate first, check reasonableness, explain reasoning (5.MP.1, MP1/MP3/MP6)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Before calculating, estimate: round the numbers to friendly ones and work it out in your head. This gives you a "should be about" number.
2. Choose a strategy that fits the numbers: split by place value, use a known fact, use the inverse, draw a model.
3. Show every step on paper so you (and the coach) can find exactly where a mistake happens.
4. After calculating, compare with the estimate. If they are far apart, something went wrong — check.
5. Explain the reasoning in a sentence using the right words: "I rounded 48 to 50 because…", "I multiplied first because…".

## Worked example
23 × 297 — Rasim got 6,847. Estimate: 20 × 300 = 6,000. 6,847 is close to 6,000 (a bit more, and 23 × 297 should be a bit more than 20 × 300), so it is reasonable. If he had written 68,470 the estimate would show it is ten times too big. Explanation: "I rounded both numbers and multiplied; the answer is near my estimate, so it is reasonable."

## Faded example (he completes)
Estimate 48 × 21: round to ___ × ___ = ___ . Now the exact answer 1,008 — is it reasonable? ___ because ___ . (Answers: 50 × 20 = 1,000; yes, it is close to 1,000.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Skipping the estimate because "I know how to do it". Scaffold: "Estimate first, every time — it takes five seconds."
- Explaining with "I just did it". Scaffold: "Tell me the first thing you did and why."
- Rounding in the wrong direction or to the wrong place. Scaffold: "Which ten is 48 nearest to?"
- Treating "reasonable" as "exactly equal". Scaffold: "Close, not equal — how close is close enough here?"

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the three-step chant — estimate, calculate, compare.
2. Picture: a number line showing the estimate and the exact answer close together (or far apart).
3. Table: problem | estimate | answer | reasonable? | why.
4. Story: shopping — "about 50 manat" before paying, then the receipt.
5. Teach it: he checks the coach's deliberately wrong answer using an estimate.

## Check by explaining back
"Why do mathematicians estimate before they calculate?"

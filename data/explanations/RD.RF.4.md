# Read grade-level text with accuracy, rate and expression — oral, timed (RD.RF.4, RF.5.4)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Fluent reading has three parts: accuracy (the right words), rate (a natural speed, not racing), and expression (your voice shows the meaning and the punctuation).
2. Read a short passage once silently to know the story; then read it aloud.
3. Punctuation is a script: a full stop means stop and breathe; a comma means a small pause; a question mark lifts your voice; quotation marks mean a character is speaking — change your voice a little.
4. If you stumble on a word, stop, fix it, and reread the whole phrase.
5. Grade-5 target: about 120–150 correct words per minute with expression, on a familiar passage. Speed is a result of accuracy, not the goal.

## Worked example
"'Step back,' said the white goat, 'because I was already on the bridge before you.'" Read: a firm, slightly bossy voice for the goat's words; a normal voice for "said the white goat"; pause at the commas; the sentence rises at "back" because it is a command. Then time a 100-word chunk: count words read, subtract errors, note the seconds.

## Faded example (he completes)
Practise this sentence three ways: flat, then with pauses at commas, then with a character voice for the quotation. Which one sounds like someone telling a story? ___ . Now do the same with the next sentence. (Answer: the third.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Racing to be fast and skipping small words. Scaffold: "Slower and right beats fast and wrong. Reread that line."
- Ignoring punctuation — one long breath. Scaffold: tap the table at every full stop.
- Monotone. Scaffold: "How does the goat feel? Make your voice show it."
- Guessing long words from the first letters. Scaffold: "Break it: ex-haus-ted."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: the three parts — accurate, steady, expressive.
2. Picture: mark the passage — slashes at pauses, arrows for voice up/down, wavy line under speech.
3. Table: date | passage | words per minute | errors | expression score (1–3), kept weekly by the parent.
4. Story: he reads a fable to a family member as a performance.
5. Teach it: he coaches a younger child to read a sentence with feeling.

## Check by explaining back
"What do the commas and quotation marks tell your voice to do?"

# Place value to 1,000,000: read, write, compare, round (G4.NBT.1, 4.NBT.A.1-3)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Numbers up to a million have two periods: thousands and ones. 406,029 is "four hundred six thousand, twenty-nine".
2. Each digit has a place and a value. In 738,462 the 3 is in the ten-thousands place and is worth 30,000.
3. To round to a place, look at the digit just to the right of that place: 5 or more → round up; less than 5 → keep. Everything to the right becomes 0.
4. To compare, line up the places; compare from the left; the first different digit decides.
5. Zeros hold places: "four hundred six thousand" has a 0 in the tens-of-thousands? No — 406 thousand: 4 hundred-thousands, 0 ten-thousands, 6 thousands.

## Worked example
Round 738,462 to the nearest ten thousand. The ten-thousands digit is 3. Look right: 8. 8 ≥ 5, so round up: 3 becomes 4, and everything to the right becomes 0 → 740,000. Check on a number line: 738,462 is between 730,000 and 740,000 and closer to 740,000.

## Faded example (he completes)
Round 52,349 to the nearest thousand. Thousands digit: ___. Digit to its right: ___. Round up or keep? ___ . Answer: ___ . (Answers: 2; 3; keep; 52,000.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Rounding the wrong place (to the nearest thousand when asked for ten thousand). Scaffold: underline the target digit first.
- "Rounding up" by changing several digits. Scaffold: "Only the target digit can change; the rest become zeros."
- Writing words as digits without placeholder zeros (46,029 for 406,029). Scaffold: "Say the thousands period as a 3-digit number: four hundred SIX = 406."
- Comparing by the first digit only. Scaffold: "Same first digit? Slide one place right."

## Representation sequence
1. Words: place names in order; the "5 or more" rule as a chant.
2. Picture: a number line between the two round numbers; where does the number sit?
3. Table: a place-value chart, the target digit circled.
4. Story: a stadium holds 52,349 people — "about how many?" for a newspaper headline.
5. Teach it: he explains rounding to a younger child using the number line.

## Check by explaining back
"How do you know whether to round up or down?"

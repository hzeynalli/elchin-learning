# Multiply multi-digit whole numbers with the standard algorithm (5.NBT.5, 5.NBT.B.5)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. Multiplying by a 2-digit number is two easier multiplications added together: 36 = 30 + 6, so 4,825 × 36 = 4,825 × 30 + 4,825 × 6.
2. Do the ones first: 4,825 × 6, working right to left and carrying.
3. Then the tens: 4,825 × 30. Because it is ×30, the row starts one place to the left — write a 0 in the ones place first, then multiply by 3.
4. Add the two rows. Estimate first (5,000 × 40 = 200,000) so you know the answer should be a bit less than that.

## Worked example
4,825 × 36. Ones row: 6 × 5 = 30, write 0 carry 3; 6 × 2 = 12 + 3 = 15, write 5 carry 1; 6 × 8 = 48 + 1 = 49, write 9 carry 4; 6 × 4 = 24 + 4 = 28. Row 1 = 28,950. Tens row: write 0, then 3 × 4,825 = 14,475, so row 2 = 144,750. Add: 28,950 + 144,750 = 173,700. Check against the estimate: close to 200,000 and a little less. Reasonable.

## Faded example (he completes)
327 × 24. Row 1: 327 × 4 = ___ . Row 2: write the 0, then 327 × 2 = 654, so the row is 6,540. Add: ___ + 6,540 = ___ . (Answers: 1,308; 7,848.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Forgetting the 0 on the tens row → the second row is ten times too small. Scaffold: "What are you really multiplying by — 3 or 30?"
- Adding the carry before multiplying (6 × 2 then +3 is right; 6 × (2 + 3) is wrong). Scaffold: "Multiply first, then add the carry."
- Lining rows up wrongly. Scaffold: use squared paper, one digit per box.
- Not estimating, so a wrong answer looks fine. Scaffold: always round both numbers first.

## Representation sequence
1. Words: "two rows, ones then tens, then add".
2. Picture: an area model — a rectangle split into 4,000 + 800 + 20 + 5 across and 30 + 6 down; each cell is a partial product.
3. Table: the partial products listed and summed.
4. Story: 36 boxes of 4,825 pencils; 30 boxes plus 6 boxes.
5. Teach it: he explains to a "younger student" why the 0 goes on the second row.

## Check by explaining back
Ask: "Why does the second row start with a zero?" A secure answer mentions tens / ×30 / ten times bigger.

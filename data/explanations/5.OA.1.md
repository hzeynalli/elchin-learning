# Evaluate expressions with parentheses, brackets, braces; order of operations (5.OA.1, 5.OA.A.1)

## The idea (in small steps)
1. An expression is a set of instructions. Everyone must follow the same order or they get different answers.
2. Grouping symbols first: ( ) inside [ ] inside { }. Work from the innermost group outwards.
3. Then × and ÷, left to right, whichever comes first.
4. Then + and −, left to right.
5. Rewrite the expression one step at a time on a new line, replacing only the part you just worked out.

## Worked example
[(18 − 6) ÷ 4] + 2 × 7. Innermost bracket: 18 − 6 = 12 → [12 ÷ 4] + 2 × 7. Square bracket: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 → 3 + 2 × 7. Multiply before add: 2 × 7 = 14 → 3 + 14 = 17.

## Faded example (he completes)
36 ÷ (2 + 4) × 5. Brackets: 2 + 4 = ___ → 36 ÷ ___ × 5. Divide (it comes first, left to right): 36 ÷ 6 = ___ → ___ × 5 = ___. (Answers: 6; 6; 30.)

## Anticipated errors (and the scaffold)
- Working strictly left to right: 8 + 4 × 3 = 36. Scaffold: "Which is stronger, × or +? Do the × first, then come back."
- Doing × before ÷ even when ÷ comes first (36 ÷ 6 × 5 as 36 ÷ 30). Scaffold: "× and ÷ are equals — read left to right."
- Dropping a bracket when rewriting. Scaffold: rewrite the whole expression each line; only replace the piece you solved.
- Confusing [ ] with a different rule. Scaffold: all grouping symbols mean "do me first"; the shapes just show nesting.

## Representation sequence
1. Words: "groups, then times and divide, then plus and minus".
2. Picture: draw a box around each group; solve from the inside box out, crossing out as you go.
3. Table: a two-column table — "expression now" | "what I did".
4. Story: a recipe where the bracket is "mix these first"; or money: 3 friends each get (2 + 5) manat.
5. Teach it: he corrects "Rasim's mistake" (5 + 3 × 2 = 16) and explains why.

## Check by explaining back
Ask him to say the order in his own words and to give one example where left-to-right would go wrong.
